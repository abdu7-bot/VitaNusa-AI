# Vita Produk — Master Roadmap 2026–2036

> **Status:** Strategic Roadmap  
> **Scope:** Vita Produk / VitaNusa-AI business direction  
> **Horizon:** 10 years  
> **Primary launch focus:** F&B digital products, followed by compliant health-product commerce  
> **Core principle:** Build a durable halal value-creation engine, not a short-term money extraction machine.

---

## 1. Vision

Vita Produk aims to become a trusted digital platform that helps people:

- buy useful and properly presented health/wellness products;
- build and operate small F&B businesses;
- use practical digital products and AI to improve productivity;
- discover trusted suppliers, experts, and business resources.

The long-term goal is a **Vita Business Operating Platform** in which AI increases productivity while humans retain appropriate control over consequential actions.

### 10-year principle

**Technology may change. The business must survive the technology change.**

Models, APIs, servers, frameworks, and vendors are replaceable. Customer trust, useful products, legitimate transactions, operational knowledge, and durable business relationships are the strategic moat.

## 4. Halal Business Constitution

Vita must prioritize value creation and honest trade.

### Required principles

1. Real product or service.
2. Clear price and transaction terms.
3. Clear ownership/rights for digital products.
4. Honest marketing and disclosure.
5. No fabricated reviews or testimonials.
6. No deceptive scarcity or manipulation.
7. No unsupported health claims.
8. No gambling/maysir mechanics.
9. No riba-based business model.
10. Avoid excessive gharar through clear terms, deliverables, refund rules, and responsibilities.
11. Protect customer data and privacy.
12. Human approval for high-risk autonomous actions.

When a specific product or financial structure raises a fiqh question, obtain qualified Islamic/legal advice rather than allowing the AI to make the final ruling.

## PHASE 8 — 2035–2036
### Vita Business Operating Platform

Target state:

> Vita helps a small business understand what is happening, decide what should happen next, and safely execute approved operational work.

Examples:

> "Your coffee cost increased 8%. Three menu items are now below the target margin."

> "Inventory suggests you may run out of packaging in four days."

> "Here are three purchasing options."

The system should recommend first, obtain approval where required, execute within bounded authority, and verify the result.

# 9. Financial Discipline

Every major feature should answer:

1. What customer problem does this solve?
2. Who pays?
3. How much value is created?
4. What does it cost Vita?
5. What is the expected gross margin?
6. How often can the value recur?
7. What operational risk does it create?

Track at minimum:
- revenue
- gross margin
- customer acquisition cost
- customer lifetime value
- conversion rate
- repeat purchase rate
- subscription retention
- refund rate
- support cost
- infrastructure cost
- AI cost per active customer

# 10. Health Commerce Guardrails

Health is a higher-risk domain.

Before publishing or selling a health product, Vita should establish a product trust workflow covering:

- seller identity
- product identity
- applicable regulatory status
- ingredients/composition where relevant
- permitted claims
- marketing content
- price
- transaction terms
- customer-facing disclosures

Vita must not allow AI to invent therapeutic claims.

For uncertain medical or regulatory questions, the system should escalate instead of guessing.

---

# 11. Governance

Autonomy must increase only when reliability increases.

### Risk levels

**Low risk**
- drafts
- calculations
- summaries

**Medium risk**
- customer communication
- operational recommendations
- inventory recommendations

**High risk**
- financial transactions
- irreversible changes
- health claims
- sensitive data actions
- external commitments

High-risk actions require stronger approval and validation.

Rollback and checkpoint mechanisms must remain fail-closed.

# 13. Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Do not build features without a customer problem.
2. Do not scale before product-market evidence.
3. Do not depend on one AI provider.
4. Do not depend on one revenue source.
5. Do not sacrifice trust for short-term sales.
6. Do not use deceptive marketing.
7. Do not make unsupported health claims.
8. Do not automate high-risk actions without appropriate approval.
9. Do not let technical debt silently become business-critical debt.
10. Keep security, backup, rollback, auditability, and governance as first-class product infrastructure.
11. Preserve customer and creator ownership/rights according to clear terms.
12. Prefer sustainable cash flow over vanity growth.

## Roadmap Review Cycle

Review this roadmap at least every 6 months.

At each review ask:

- What assumptions became false?
- What customers actually paid for?
- Which product has the strongest retention?
- Which revenue stream has the healthiest margin?
- Which technology dependency became risky?
- Which regulatory requirements changed?
- What should be stopped?
- What should be doubled down on?

The roadmap may change.

The core principles — **halal value creation, customer trust, financial discipline, replaceable technology, and safe automation** — should change much more slowly.
